# CONTRIBUTING — Humanity OS

Humanity OS is intentionally incomplete. Criticism is a feature, not a defect.

## Contribution Standard

Contributions should be nonviolent, evidence-aware, explicit about assumptions and trade-offs, respectful of human dignity and minority rights, auditable, and open to criticism and revision.

Disagreement with the current text is welcome.

## Ways to Contribute

You may identify contradictions, loopholes, or unintended consequences; propose amendments; submit counter-evidence; improve definitions or metrics; translate or localize an edition; document linguistic divergences; propose alternative institutional implementations; red-team the specification for abuse, capture, corruption, centralization, or AI failure; or create a documented fork.

## Language Contributions

No language is the language of authority. A translation should preserve the principle, right, duty, limitation, and degree of uncertainty in the reference text rather than merely reproduce words literally.

When a term has no exact equivalent: document the ambiguity; explain the translation choice; preserve alternative interpretations where useful; and invite review by speakers familiar with the relevant legal and cultural context. Meaningful divergences must not be silently resolved.

## Safety and Nonviolence

Do not use this project to coordinate violence, terrorism, harassment, doxxing, illegal access to systems, forced conversion, indiscriminate destruction, persecution, or collective retaliation.

## Public AI Contributions

Proposals involving Public AI should specify, where relevant: operator and funding model; model/provider dependencies; data sources; decision authority; auditability; appeal process; privacy safeguards; cybersecurity assumptions; human oversight; independent AI oversight; failure modes; and rollback procedure.

Public AI should assist detection, explanation, comparison, and audit; it should not become an unauditable final authority.

## Review Standard

A proposal should not be accepted merely because it sounds compassionate, efficient, democratic, or technologically advanced. It should be examined for measurable outcomes, abuse cases, capture risk, minority-rights impact, long-term effects, reversibility, implementation cost, evidence quality, and unintended incentives.

**Read it. Critique it. Test it. Fork it. Improve it.**
